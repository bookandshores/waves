Six Strange Ones

A samurai on the sand, a UFO over the pier, and the six most unusual books in our Japanese Literature collection: ghosts, curses, an ominous old map and a grim reaper who looks like Orson Welles.

Illustrations by A. E. Goodson for Book and Shores.

No. 1
Platonic Love Mieko Kanai, tr. Polly Barton

A cat who looks like Orson Welles and is the grim reaper, a murder in a high-school secret society of knights, and a truly disturbing tale of rabbits and fathers.

Preorder · Apr 2027 The details →
No. 2
Caring for a Corpse Harumi Miura, tr. Tristan Hill

A live-in caregiver in a mountain mansion, and her charge: bluish-green skin, a black bag over her head, and, she swears, already dead.

Preorder · Jan 2027 The details →
No. 3
Six: Paths of Horror Nashi

Six doors into death: a rooftop amusement park, a 23 minute and 45 second film, the corpse of a ghost, sounds echoing through an elevator.

Out now The details →
No. 4
Cursed Thing Nashi, tr. Jim Rion

Fake-documentary horror built from forum posts, creepypastas, email chains, tampered audio, and one woman’s face littered across the city.

Preorder · Feb 2027 The details →
No. 5
Strange Maps Uketsu, tr. Jim Rion

A grandmother found clutching an old map of a village, a lake, a mountain and yokai, and a tunnel steeped in ill fortune.

Preorder · Apr 2027 The details →
No. 6
The Museum of Silence Yoko Ogawa, tr. Stephen Snyder

A young curator builds a museum of stolen objects, each one taken from someone who died in the town.

Preorder · Jun 2027 The details →
