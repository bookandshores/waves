The Holiday Gift Guide

Books for everyone on your list, especially the ones who swear they don't want anything. They do. They want a book. Here are twenty-seven, sorted by the kind of person who'll unwrap them, and every one ships from an independent bookstore. Tap a cover to buy. Some titles open into our longer take.

Updated October 2026

Still stuck? The Booker 2026 shortlist has six good bets, our Author of the Month has a whole shelf, and the bookstore has everything else.

For the One Who Stays Through the Credits

Start with the film people, because they're the hardest. They've seen everything. They haven't read all of these.

The Edges of Cinema — Erika Balsom

Film critic Erika Balsom on the cinema happening at the margins. For the friend who waits until the last name scrolls by.

Buy Now
A Sudden Flicker of Light — David Thomson

One of film's great critics rewrites the history of the movies. Expect arguments over dessert.

Buy Now
The Friday Afternoon Club — Griffin Dunne

Griffin Dunne's family memoir, in his own words: “From the moment I was born, my father told me I was always trying to get somewhere else. My first word was taxi.”

Buy Now
The Great Eastern — Howard A. Rodman

Captain Nemo versus Captain Ahab on the high seas. Rodman makes it an anticolonial adventure, and a witty one.

Buy Now

Highsmith for the Holidays

Speaking of movies, Patricia Highsmith has supplied plenty. Envy, glamour and a Christmas doll: buy both and let them fight it out under the tree.

The Talented Mr. Ripley — Patricia Highsmith

Tom Ripley falls for Dickie Greenleaf's sunlit life on the Italian coast, then helps himself to it. Pair it with Helen Mirren playing Highsmith in A Talent for Murder.

Buy Now
Carol — Patricia Highsmith

A department-store clerk and a glamorous suburban mother fall in love over a Christmas doll, then take to the open road. Possibly the most December book ever written.

Buy Now

For the Record Collector

Next, the person who flips the record before it ends and has opinions about the bootleg.

Music Sets You Free — Ryuichi Sakamoto

Ryuichi Sakamoto, in his own words: “What I want to make now is music freed from the constraints of time.” Play something quiet while you read.

Buy Now
Live Dead — John Brackett

John Brackett on the Grateful Dead, live recordings and the ideology of liveness. For tapers and thinkers, who are often the same person.

Buy Now
Touch of Grey — John Brackett

Brackett again, on how the Dead became pop stars. A long, strange trip to the charts.

Buy Now
The Art of Stanley Mouse: California Dreams — Stanley Mouse

Psychedelic poster art from the hand behind classic Grateful Dead imagery. The coffee table just got louder.

Buy Now
US v. THEM — Ronen Givony

Ronen Givony on the age of indie music and a decade in New York, 2004 to 2014. Loud, and a little nostalgic.

Buy Now
Madame Composer — Sarah Fritz

Sarah Fritz puts Clara Schumann's virtuosic genius center stage, where it belonged all along.

Buy Now

Something Wicked in the Stocking

Then there's the friend who reads with the lights on. Crime, secrets and at least one bad decision on Lake Como.

Black Dahlia — William J. Mann

Murder, monsters and madness in midcentury Hollywood. As William J. Mann puts it: “The cops were being played, likely by more than one player…”

Buy Now
A Quiet Place — Seichō Matsumoto

Seichō Matsumoto sets the trap calmly: “Tsuneo Asai was on a business trip to the Kansai region when he heard the news.” It doesn't stay quiet for long.

Buy Now
I Made You Up Inside My Head — Marta Pérez-Carbonell

A sleeper train, a translator and a literary scandal where fiction and betrayal blur. Best read on an overnight trip, if you dare.

Buy Now
The Plunge — Lila Raicek

Desire, betrayal and a dangerous love triangle, from Manhattan to Lake Como. A summer thriller, which is exactly why it works in December.

Buy Now

For the Coast-Minded

For the friend who'd rather be near the water: salt water, California water, and the end of the world on a beach in Kent.

Tin Can Coast — Joseph Ogilvy

Joseph Ogilvy on industry, greed and fishing in the Golden State, and everything the California coast pulled from the sea.

Buy Now
Aqua — Chiara Barzini

Chiara Barzini follows the Los Angeles Aqueduct from Owens Valley to the Salton Sea. Water, Hollywood and California's lost dreams.

Buy Now
The End of Everything — M. John Harrison

A beachcomber scrapes by on the coast of Kent while the world comes apart. Booker-shortlisted, slyly funny and properly strange.

Buy Now

For the Serious Reader

Now the reader who already owns every tote bag. Skip the bag. Bring the book.

The Things We Never Say — Elizabeth Strout

Booker-shortlisted Elizabeth Strout, at her gentlest: “It was the middle of June and the sun all day had kept right on shining with sweet mightiness.”

Buy Now
John of John — Douglas Stuart

A young man goes home to his strict father on a Calvinist island in the Outer Hebrides. Booker-shortlisted, from the author of Shuggie Bain.

Buy Now
A Different Kind of Tension — Jonathan Lethem

Jonathan Lethem's new and selected stories, bending genre the way only he can.

Buy Now
The Best American Essays 2026 — M. Gessen, guest editor

Twenty essays from 2025, chosen by guest editor M. Gessen. Open it anywhere; it'll be smart about it.

Buy Now

Stocking Stuffers and the Kitchen Table

Last, the little ones that fit in a stocking or live by the stove. Small, funny, well-loved and edible-adjacent.

Moosewood Cookbook — Mollie Katzen

The classic vegetarian cookbook, hand-lettered and much loved. Every kitchen needs a copy, especially the messy ones.

Buy Now
Snoopy and the Red Baron — Charles M. Schulz

One beagle, one doghouse, one sworn enemy. “Curse you, Red Baron!”

Buy Now
The Land and Its People — David Sedaris

David Sedaris asks the important questions: “If you had a year, do you think you could eat one of those?”

Buy Now
Cool Machine — Colson Whitehead

Colson Whitehead's Ray Carney, a man with priorities: “It's harder to find a good coffee table than it is to fall in love.”

Buy Now

Still Can't Choose? Spin Brigitte.

One spin, one book, no dithering. Brigitte has impeccable taste and zero patience for “maybe the other one.”
