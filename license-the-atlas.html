The Book & Shores Atlas is Earth's curated atlas of books, each pinned to its stated setting and sourced, made by Book & Shores, an independent bookstore. Publishers, authors, artists and illustrators, festivals, libraries and tourism boards can put their books and their places on it.

What it does

  • Type any city, country or region and see the books set there.
  • Each pin opens a card with the cover, the setting and the source for it, plus print and ebook links.
  • When a book names a region instead of an address, a dashed ring shows the area, so nothing is overstated.
  • Reading routes link stops in order: Gothic October, the National Book Awards 2026 and the Booker Prize 2026.
  • Works on phones, and every pin is also listed in plain text below the map.

Why put a book on the map

  • Readers look for books by place. Each place on the Atlas gets its own plain page, "Books set in Savannah, Georgia", written so search engines and AI answer tools can read it and cite it.
  • A pin doesn't expire. Most print sales come from backlist books, about 70% in the US (Circana BookScan). A pin keeps a book findable long after launch season.
  • Every pin is sourced. The setting is cited from the book, its publisher or a major review, and partner pins are labeled. That's what makes a pin worth trusting and worth citing.
  • Place brings people. A route through the settings of a festival lineup or a region's books gives readers somewhere to go, and gives the place its books.

Who it's for

  • University, small, indie and art-book presses: a sourced pin for each new title, linked back to your catalog page. A Regional Atlas for a state or city list.
  • Authors, including debut and self-published: a Launch Pin. If the book or its own description names the place, it can be pinned. A pin is not a review, and our takes are never for sale.
  • Artists and illustrators: a Launch Pin for an illustrated or art book set somewhere real, credited by name. A Partner Map for a show or a series.
  • Literary festivals and organizations: a Partner Map of your lineup's settings, with its own page and route.
  • Libraries and tourism boards: a Regional Atlas of the books set in your place.

The rules stay the same

We only pin what we can verify. A partner pin needs the same source as any other pin: the book, its publisher or a major review naming the place.

Partner pins and maps are always marked as partner work. Our takes on books are never for sale.
