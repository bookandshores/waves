Long Take

Akira Kurosawa, translated by Anne McKnight

By Akira Kurosawa, translated by Anne McKnight • University of Minnesota Press • 240 pages • 32 black-and-white illustrations • Published 3 February 2026 • ISBN 9781517903299 (paperback); 9781517903282 (hardcover) • Out now

University of Minnesota Press calls this a multifaceted portrait of the director, now available to English readers for the first time, in Anne McKnight's translation. First published in Japan shortly after Kurosawa's death in 1998, it takes up the story of his life after Rashomon, the point where his earlier memoir, Something like an Autobiography, left off.

The press draws the portrait from Kurosawa's own writings; his conversations with writer Inoue Hisashi and director Yamada Yōji; and essays by his daughter and colleague Kurosawa Kazuko. It includes an annotated list of Kurosawa's 100 favorite films.

A Weekly Reads pick for November 15, on Seoul to Tokyo on the B&S Literary Atlas. A portrait of the director, with no single place named, so the book is listed with the route, not pinned. Go There. Read It.
